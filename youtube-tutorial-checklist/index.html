How to make a checklist from a YouTube tutorial

Turn a how-to video into a short sequence you can follow while your hands are on the project. Here is a method that works with a notes app or SaySo.

  • Prepare first
  • One action per step
  • Keep visual cues

SaySo keeps a guided task visible while you work.

A five-step method that works with any tutorial

Make the list useful at the moment you are doing the work.

1. Scan the whole process

Watch the video once, or use its chapters and transcript if available. Note the goal, the tools or ingredients, and any steps that must happen in a fixed order. Do not copy the creator's introduction or commentary into your checklist.

2. Put preparation first

Start with the things you need before the first action: materials, workspace setup, and any waiting time. This keeps an easy-to-miss prerequisite from interrupting you halfway through.

3. Write one action per line

Begin each item with a verb such as gather, cut, attach, stir, or check. Split a long instruction when it asks you to do two separate things. Keep the wording short enough to understand at a glance.

4. Save visual cues

Add a timestamp beside a step when you need to see a hand position, measurement, or technique again. The checklist is for sequence; the video remains the source for details you should not guess.

5. Review before starting

Read the list from top to bottom and fix missing or out-of-order actions. If a step depends on something unclear in the video, check the original tutorial before treating the checklist as complete.

Quick checklist test

  • Can you identify everything to gather first?
  • Does each item describe one action?
  • Are the steps in the order you will do them?
  • Do tricky steps point back to the video?

Example: a button-sewing tutorial

The exact technique comes from the video; the checklist keeps the actions in view.

  1. Gather the button, needle, thread, scissors, and garment.
  2. Thread the needle and secure the thread.
  3. Place the button where it belongs.
  4. Stitch through the button holes as the tutorial demonstrates. Replay the technique segment if needed.
  5. Secure the thread and trim the end.
  6. Check that the button is attached and can be used comfortably.

This is an illustrative outline, not a replacement for the original tutorial. A more complex project may also need measurements, safety notes, or checkpoints between steps.

Reusable template

  • Gather materials and tools
  • Prepare the workspace
  • Do the first action
  • Repeat for each action
  • Check the result
  • Clean up or store materials

Questions about video checklists

How do I turn a YouTube tutorial into a checklist?

Watch or scan it once, write the actions in order, put tools and materials first, and add timestamps where you need to see a detail again. On iPhone, you can share the video to SaySo and review the generated steps before starting.

What if the video has no transcript or chapters?

Watch it in sections and pause at each change of action. Write a short verb-led step for each action, then add a timestamp for anything you will need to see again.

Does a checklist replace the original video?

No. Keep the tutorial available for technique, measurements, and safety details. The checklist helps you keep your place in the sequence.
